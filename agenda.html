One Conversation. Every Room.™Opening

Human Ingenuity and the Future of Leadership in the Age of AI

Leadership Wall Unveiling & Opening Address

Dr. Maureen Dunne

+Read moreClose

What if the perspective your organization most needs belongs to someone it has never learned to hear?

As leaders invest in artificial intelligence, an equally consequential question demands attention: are we creating the conditions for human intelligence to flourish? Drawing on her latest research into cognitive diversity in the age of AI, Dr. Maureen Dunne opens the Summit with a challenge to rethink cognitive infrastructure—the environments, relationships, and technologies that shape whose abilities are recognized, whose ideas influence decisions, and what we learn from one another. She explores how different minds and AI can complement one another, and why judgment, empathy, imagination, and learning across generations belong at the heart of leadership.

One Conversation. Every Room. brings this vision to life. Conceived as a living laboratory, the Summit invites people to experience Davos on their own terms, with personalized technology, supports, and adaptive environments—and the freedom to come and go, pause, and participate in ways that suit them. Through small experiments in collaboration, participants across neurotypes and generations will explore new possibilities for learning and work. Unveiling the Leadership Wall and introducing the Summit’s book project, Dunne asks how what we discover together can become part of our institutions—and part of what we pass on.

We need all kinds of minds. The responsibility of leadership is to ensure that what they see, know, and imagine has a chance to shape our shared future.

Whose Mind Are We Designing For?Fireside

Keynote Fireside Chat: Cognitive Aesthetics and the Future of Accessible Design

Daria Tsoupikova and Kay Sargent

Moderated by Dr. Maureen Dunne

+Read moreClose

Every environment makes assumptions about the mind experiencing it. What happens when those assumptions are wrong?

In this keynote fireside chat, cognitive scientist Dr. Maureen Dunne moderates a conversation with immersive art and virtual reality pioneer Daria Tsoupikova and Kay Sargent of HOK on the emerging frontier of cognitive aesthetics. Bringing a fresh lens to decades of cognitive science research, the discussion explores how perception, attention, emotion, and sensory processing shape our experience of beauty, comfort, and belonging. From architecture and workplaces to immersive digital worlds, discover how designing for different minds can open new possibilities for creativity, agency, and participation—and challenge whose experience defines “good design” in the first place.

What Success Doesn’t ShowFireside

Leadership, Trust, and the Hidden Cost of Fitting In

Adam Livesey and Ben Idle, Deutsche Bank

Moderated by Tristan Lavender

+Read moreClose

When performance looks strong, what might a leader still be missing?

In this intimate fireside conversation, Adam Livesey and Ben Idle, senior leaders at Deutsche Bank with lived experience of neurodivergence, join moderator Tristan Lavender, neurodiversity leader and founder of the Philips Neurodiversity Network, to explore what conventional measures of success may leave unseen: the effort of masking, the impact of discovering your neurodivergence later in life, and the difference it makes when a colleague genuinely listens.

Drawing on their personal experiences and neuroinclusion work at Deutsche Bank, they discuss how an initiative gains organizational backing, why visible senior leadership matters, and which everyday behaviors can make it easier for people to contribute as themselves. For leaders responsible for talent, culture, and performance, the question is immediate: how much energy do your people spend fitting in—and what could become possible if more of that energy were available for the work they care about?

Who Gets to Shape What Comes Next?Conversation

An intergenerational conversation on mental health, enterprise, and opportunity in the age of AI

Moderated by members of the World Economic Forum’s Global Shapers Community

Panelists to be announced

+Read moreClose

If technology expands what is possible, why do so many people still struggle to find a way in?

Access to mental health support, the chance to build a business, and a voice in decisions can determine whether someone’s potential has room to grow. This intergenerational conversation brings Global Shapers and experienced leaders together to explore how AI, investment, and social innovation could open those doors—and what it takes to earn the trust of the people they are intended to serve.

The discussion asks what different generations can learn from one another about well-being, ambition, and the systems we inherit. For executives, it offers a practical challenge: how can your organization help widen access to care, enterprise, and meaningful work while giving people a greater say in shaping them? The next generation will inherit today’s decisions. What could change if generations made them together?

Leading with Humanity in the Age of AIConversation

A Workplace Where Every Mind Can Thrive

Speakers to be announced

+Read moreClose

As AI becomes more widely available, could the quality of your human culture become your greatest competitive advantage?

This conversation explores how leaders can navigate technological change while creating workplaces where people across neurotypes feel trusted, understood, and able to contribute. From bridging empathy and communication gaps to helping teams learn from different perspectives, the discussion examines the everyday leadership choices that shape whether innovation takes root—or valuable ideas go unheard.

Connecting corporate practice, education, and cross-sector partnerships, it asks how organizations can prepare people for changing work while protecting the relationships that make collaboration possible. Within the Summit’s living laboratory, leaders across generations will consider how personalized technology and human-centered cultures can reinforce one another. When technology changes what we can do, leadership determines what we can achieve together.

A Life of PossibilityConversation

An Intimate Conversation about Work, Family, and Belonging

Michael Schwallie and Cathy Schwallie Farmer, with Wawa’s employer perspective

+Read moreClose

For a parent, the question may be: will my child have opportunities to build a life they love? For an employer: how can we recognize someone’s strengths and create the conditions for them to thrive?

This warm, personal conversation brings those hopes and responsibilities together through the experiences of Michael Schwallie, his sister and advocate Cathy Schwallie Farmer, and Wawa’s perspective as an employer. From meaningful work and supportive colleagues to Special Olympics, travel, and friendships, Michael shares the people, experiences, and accomplishments that matter to him.

Cathy reflects on family, advocacy, and opening doors, while Wawa explores the everyday practices that help turn an employment opportunity into a lasting relationship. A conversation about what becomes possible when families, employers, and communities work together—and when people have a voice in shaping the lives they want to lead.

Protecting Every Mind: The Next Frontier of Corporate TrustDinner & Fireside

Neural Data, Human Agency, and Leadership in the Intelligent Age

Stephen Damianos, NeuroRights Foundation

Moderated by Dr. Maureen Dunne

+Read moreClose

Business leaders are asking what AI can do. A more intimate question is emerging: what happens when technology draws on data from the human brain?

Over dinner, join Stephen Damianos of the NeuroRights Foundation and cognitive scientist and moderator Dr. Maureen Dunne for a keynote fireside conversation at the intersection of neurotechnology, cognitive diversity, and corporate responsibility. Drawing on Safeguarding Brain Data, this discussion brings neural data privacy into dialogue with the science of how differently people think, perceive, and process information.

Explore the choices leaders face as innovation tests the boundaries of consent, privacy, and human agency—and why safeguards built around an assumed “average” mind may fall short. Who gets to define those boundaries, whose minds might be overlooked, and what should CEOs be asking before these technologies enter their products and workplaces?
