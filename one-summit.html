DNS 2027 · A Davos gathering, reimagined

One conversation. Every room.

Most summits ask every guest to take in the program the same way: one room, one stage, sit still. DNS 2027 carries one live conversation into every room, so each guest chooses how to take part, moment by moment, and no one misses anything by choosing.

Two ways to hold a summit

Most events ask you to fit the room. This one fits you.

The difference is not an amenity added to a standard format. It is a different format.

How a standard summit compares with DNS 2027
The standard summit DNS 2027
Where you are One room, facing one stage. Any room. The conversation reaches all of them.
How you take part Sit still, stay quiet, face forward. Sit close, pace, listen alone, or talk by the fire.
Stepping away Means missing what was said. Costs nothing. You are still in it.
If you need something different Ask, explain, and hope. Nothing to ask for. It is already there, for everyone.
Who gets heard The front row. Any room, by phone, through a host who speaks to every room.

How it works

The thinking behind it

Designed from the research, not around it.

Every choice in this format traces back to what the research says makes inclusion real rather than cosmetic.

01

No penalty for choosing.

At most events, stepping out costs you the content and the room's attention. Here it costs nothing. The confidence that being yourself carries no penalty is the foundation every other kind of inclusion stands on.

Psychological safety
02

Nothing to ask for.

What is necessary for some is available to all. No one has to disclose, request, or explain anything to take part in their own way. That is the difference between an accommodation and a design.

Universal design
03

A voice from any room.

Groups converge quickly, and the most useful question in the building is often never asked from the front row. Opening the conversation to every room, and every way of thinking, is a guard against groupthink.

Cognitive diversity

These principles come from two decades of research and practice by DNS founder Dr. Maureen Dunne, set out in her national bestseller The Neurodiversity Edge. About the founder →

Why it matters beyond the room

The argument, made in the room.

DNS exists to show the world's leading organizations that authentic neuroinclusion is a strategic asset: a source of innovation at a moment when technology is rewriting the terms of success faster than anyone can plan for.

This year, the summit does more than make that case. It is designed to demonstrate it. Different minds, given room to work their own way, make a better gathering than the standard format does. Guests will not only hear the argument. They will spend the day inside it.

It isn't the whole solution to anything. But it is part of the solution to nearly everything.

Dr. Maureen Dunne, on neuroinclusion · The Neurodiversity Edge
